# Mission Rules: Communications

Ares Base flight rules, section C. How and when the crew talks to Mission Control.

## Comm delay table
One-way signal time between Mars and Earth:
| Earth to Mars distance | One-way delay | Round trip |
|---|---|---|
| Closest approach, 55 million km | 3 minutes | 6 minutes |
| Average, 225 million km | 12.5 minutes | 25 minutes |
| Sol 212, 380 million km | 21 minutes | 42 minutes |
| Farthest, 400 million km | up to 24 minutes | up to 48 minutes |
Add about 5 minutes for Mission Control to read and answer. On Sol 212 a question takes 47 minutes to come back.

## Blackout windows
No link while Mars is below the relay horizon: 02:10 to 05:40 local time each sol.
No link for about 14 sols at solar conjunction. The next conjunction starts on Sol 388.

## What must be reported to Mission Control
Report within 1 hour:
- Cabin CO2 above 4.0 mmHg, or any scrubber fault light.
- Cabin pressure under 65 kPa.
- Battery state of charge under 40 percent.
- Any injury or illness.
- Sorbent margin under 60 days.
Do not wait for a reply before acting. Follow the manual first, then report.

## How to report: the incident report
Send an incident report by email to Mission Control at missioncontrol@aresbase.example.
Subject line: Incident report, Sol number, system name.
The body must give three things:
1. Reading: the measured value with units.
2. Time: local mission time of the reading.
3. Action taken: what the crew did, and the next recheck time.
Keep it under 100 words. The crew member on shift reviews and sends it.

## Medical contact
Medical questions go to the flight surgeon, Dr. Lee, at dr.lee@aresbase.example.
Private medical conferences are on Sols ending in 0 and 5, at 14:00 local time.

## Daily report
Send the daily status by 20:00 local time: CO2 reading, sorbent beds remaining, battery state of charge, water tank level.
